crshhx · design research · phase 2

Three directions, built to be looked at.

Each concept below is a real, working HTML page — not a description and not a mockup image. Open them, scroll them, resize the window, and judge them the way you would judge a live site. All three use genuine crshhx copy and real product data: 258 presets, five weapon slots, v1.1.0, 84.7 MB and 77.7 MB, both SHA-256 digests, F10 and F9. Nothing is invented and no testimonial, customer count or award appears.

Two caveats before you look. These pages load their display faces from Google Fonts so each direction can be judged with the typeface it actually intends — that link must never ship, because the production site self-hosts woff2 subsets and the build now fails on any non-first-party origin. And the crosshairs you see are drawn by hand here as static SVG; in the real site they come from the existing Reticle.tsx renderer, which mirrors the application's own crosshair schema, and the interactive editor in the hero is the existing Playground.tsx.
Direction A

Aperture — immersive dark, product-lit

One dark room, one lit subject. A 92px display headline against a large square stage holding the reticle at scale, with a monospace readout. Capabilities are presented as a specification rail with no borders and no cards — separated by space alone — then two alternating product sections. The accent appears maybe six times on the whole page.

Strengths

  • Closest to Linear and Raycast's register
  • Every reticle colour is legible
  • Cheapest to build from the current code
  • Reads as a serious tool, not a game mod

Weaknesses

  • Nearest to the look you called generic dark dev-docs
  • Least distinctive of the three
  • Depends entirely on execution to avoid flatness
Direction B

Paper & Precision — light editorial, dark plates

A technical monograph. Warm paper, ink-black text, a high-contrast serif display at up to 124px, sections numbered like a journal (01 / Designer), specification lists ruled rather than boxed, and presets as a real table instead of a card grid. The product appears on dark plates — the only dark surfaces on the page, which is precisely why they hold the eye.

Strengths

  • Genuinely unlike every gaming-adjacent site
  • Best long-form readability for the documentation
  • Editorial confidence reads as established
  • Dark plates make the product a focal point

Weaknesses

  • A serif display is a strong, less safe choice
  • Light chrome can feel less "software"
  • Two surface worlds to maintain consistently
Direction C

Field & Studio — split contrast, one seam

Built on a fact about the product rather than a style: crshhx exists between two worlds — the game, which is dark and full-screen, and the editor, which is precise and light. The hero splits down the middle, field on the left and studio on the right, with the crosshair sitting exactly on the seam. Bands then alternate world down the page, and the download panel is split the same way.

Strengths

  • The most original and most memorable
  • The concept is true to the product, not applied to it
  • Strong graphic identity, instantly recognisable
  • Solves light-vs-dark by using both deliberately

Weaknesses

  • Highest build and maintenance cost
  • The split collapses to a stack on mobile
  • Bold type can tip into loud if overused

Recommendation

C as the identity, executed with A's restraint. Direction C is the only one of the three whose form comes from something true about crshhx rather than from a style preference, which is what makes it look designed instead of styled — and it answers your objection directly, because a light studio half is the opposite of generic dark developer documentation. But C alone risks being loud, so it should borrow A's discipline: the accent stays rare, no section repeats a layout, and the split is used as a structural idea at maybe three moments on the homepage rather than as decoration on every band. Direction B's editorial rigour — ruled specification lists, presets as a table, numbered sections — is the right treatment for the documentation and changelog regardless of which hero wins.
